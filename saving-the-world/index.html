Why we are building this

There's a difference between building a business and personally financing everything the business needs to survive. Jungle Star's next chapter is about creating durable operations: customers, contracts, staffing, inventory, training, facilities, transparent finances, and measurable community benefit.

Our guiding principle is resilience is not recovery. Resilience keeps a founder moving through crisis; recovery builds systems that can operate without constant personal sacrifice. Our proposed model connects publishing, entertainment, technology, food service, manufacturing and education instead of making every good program depend on the next donation.

What does “100% of my personal proceeds” mean?

The founder's stated commitment is to dedicate 100% of his personal creator proceeds from these named educational titles toward Jungle Star's creative and community mission. This is a founder pledge, not a statement that 100% of gross book prices are charitable donations or that all company revenue is given away.

Before publishing a calculated total, Jungle Star will define eligible titles and proceeds, document printing and distribution costs, reconcile royalties actually received, and publish how those proceeds were used. These purchases are product sales; they are not automatically tax-deductible charitable gifts.

Four proposed operating concepts · One connected mission

The $1 million development initiative

Our development scenario is designed to test four complementary enterprises and a shared support system, with phased site selection based on actual lease costs, local demand, zoning, permits, operating reserves and cash flow. No funding or property acquisition is implied by this plan.

01 · Little Legends Arcade & Build-A-Better-World Academy — $250,000 scenario

For children and families: affordable games, literacy, STEM, robotics, 3D printing, toy design, storytelling, educational entertainment, parties, books and school/community partnerships.

Earned revenue: tickets, memberships, workshops, birthday parties, maker activities, educational contracts, games, merchandise and concessions if permitted.

Early execution: establish the minimum viable floor plan, accessible equipment, safeguarding and supervision, staffing ratios, per-visit cost, enrollment capacity and three-to-six-month operating reserve before committing to a location.

02 · Isaac's Phried Chicken and specialty foods — $175,000 scenario

Restore and expand the founder's food-business experience through a restaurant or a lower-capital permitted mobile pilot, selected according to available funds, demand and site economics. Menu opportunities include chicken, produce-based sides, soups, sauces, Barker Family Brew tea, and culinary education.

Two pilot paths: (A) lease and fit out a properly permitted kitchen or restaurant with proven foot traffic; (B) begin with a licensed food truck or permitted pop-up supported by an approved kitchen. Truck procurement, inspection, utilities, equipment, staffing, storage, insurance and operating reserves must be quoted.

Whole-chicken preparation, broth production, food reuse and meals for community distribution require approved food-safety processes and handling plans.

03 · Jungle Star City — $225,000 scenario

An adult-oriented entertainment and maker venue for gaming, performances, private parties, creator events, fabrication activities, merchandising and community enterprise. Separate child and adult operations and programming carefully.

Community Housing & Occupational Alliance (CHOA): proposed administrative and partnership program for practical workforce referral, career pathways, entrepreneurship and housing-resource navigation. CHOA is not represented as currently licensed housing or placement services. Alcohol-related operations are optional, require separate analysis and licenses, and are not an assumption behind the base revenue plan.

04 · Community Food Processing & Meal Center — $250,000 scenario

A proposed food-production hub supporting meals for paying customers and separately funded community distribution, culinary jobs, packaging, preservation and contract processing. Possible product lines include seasonings, teas, sauces, soups, refrigerated meals, frozen produce, jams, juices, ice pops and prepared ingredients.

Potential customers include restaurants, families, events, institutional buyers and partner programs. Farm partnerships, supply agreements, food waste prevention and local sourcing are central to the plan.

Safety: meat processing, ready-to-eat meals, refrigerated transport, shelf-stable canned goods, acidified products and juices may each require different permits, process validation, facility controls and specialized oversight. No home-canning method is an acceptable substitute for commercial regulatory review.

05 · Shared administration, legal and contingency — $100,000 scenario

Entity review, accounting, site feasibility, permitting, insurance, agreements, governance, purchasing support and contingency. This provisional amount is not proof that reserves for four operating venues would be adequate. Working capital must be modeled separately for each launch stage.

Food that feeds the business—and the neighborhood

Sell

Earned sales from prepared meals, catering, drinks and properly licensed packaged food lines.

Train

Paid staff, apprentices and culinary trainees learn safe, repeatable commercial production.

Serve

Partner-funded meals and reduced-cost community distributions, accounted for separately from sales.

Proposed recurring model: each community meal has a transparent food, labor, packaging and delivery cost. Partner contributions or a documented business subsidy cover that cost. The goal is dependable capacity rather than promising unlimited free meals.

Local sourcing principle: identify gaps in demand and local supply first; seek purchasing contracts, co-packing and distribution partnerships with established farms and producers before duplicating their facilities. New farms should complement existing producers where feasible, strengthen local jobs and compete fairly—not be built solely to displace neighbors.

A ten-year vision: 1,000 sustainable employment opportunities

Our long-term founder goal is to help support at least 1,000 paid, durable work opportunities within ten years through operating venues, franchising where legally feasible, food supply, manufacturing, youth careers and partner-led ventures.

What we will measure: direct jobs, average weekly hours, wages, retention, partner jobs, paid apprenticeships and business creation. A short workshop, volunteer shift or one-off gig will not be mislabeled a sustained full-time job.

Expansion requires revenue to cover wages and working costs. A planned franchise is not yet a registered franchise offering, and projected jobs are not hiring announcements.

Community partnerships and a lasting Peace Prize

Jungle Star envisions distinct, future initiatives such as a Jungle Star Kids Peace Prize recognizing young people and their positive community ideas, and a separate Jungle Star Peace Prize for broader contributions to community life. Selection rules, funding, governance and eligibility are not yet established. Prize funds will not be described as endowed or guaranteed until formally funded and governed.

The larger vision includes schools, community gardens, makers, restaurants, libraries, filmmakers, farms, manufacturers, social-service organizations and neighbors—contributing expertise through clear written arrangements.

Questions people ask

Are all four locations open?
No. These are proposed operating concepts with a preliminary development budget. Existing books, creative works, community programming and a playable demo are separate from the planned facilities.
Is the $1 million already raised?
No. It is a proposed funding and development scenario, not reported cash received or committed.
Are purchases and sponsorships tax deductible?
A product purchase is not automatically a charitable contribution. No tax-deductible treatment is asserted here; legal entity status and any eligible donations must be verified separately.
Can my business become part of the game?
Potentially, through carefully reviewed educational mission collaborations, licensed content, workshops and sponsorships with written creative deliverables and child-appropriate disclosures. Inclusion is not guaranteed.
Can I pre-order the game?
The public demo is available now. Any commercial preorder will require a verified checkout and disclosed platforms, fulfillment expectations and refund terms; this page does not accept preorders.
Does Jungle Star already operate a franchise system?
No franchise offerings are made through this page. The four-location vision is a development framework, not a currently authorized franchise sale.
How will community meals be funded?
A future food program would combine commercial meal sales with separately funded community distribution agreements. Meal quantities and recipients will only be announced when production, delivery and costs have been confirmed.
