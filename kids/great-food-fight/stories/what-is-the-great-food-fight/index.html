WHAT IS THE GREAT FOOD FIGHT?

You thought this was just about broccoli fighting candy?

FOUNDER APPROVED • CLEARED 2026-10-01

NARRATOR

Okay. Quick question.

What exactly is The Great Food Fight?

Is it vegetables fighting candy? Yes.

Is it fruit carrying weapons that probably should require paperwork? Also yes.

Is there a watermelon who becomes a king? Absolutely.

Is there a broccoli general? Unfortunately for his enemies... yes.

But if that's all you see, you're only looking at the first layer.

Because The Great Food Fight is really about a much bigger question: What happens when a kid learns how the world around them actually works?

SCENE 2 — FOOD IS THE FRONT DOOR

NARRATOR

Food is where the adventure begins.

Fruit. Vegetables. Water. Roots. Herbs. Soup. Recipes. Gardens. Sugar. Mystery meat. Bad choices. Good choices. Funny choices. Choices you probably should not make twice.

The game does not start by telling a child, “Sit down. Today we will study nutrition.” Because that's how you lose half the room.

Instead, the broccoli has armor. The watermelon has a destiny. The ginger moves like a ninja. The corn has family problems.

And suddenly, everybody wants to know what happens next.

SCENE 3 — THE FOOD HAS PERSONALITY

DEORUM

So why does the broccoli have to be a general?

ISAAC

Because broccoli ain't just broccoli anymore. He's got history. He's got family. He's got stuff he's trying to protect.

GLITCHBOT

Character principle detected: Children remember lessons better when the lesson has somebody they care about attached to it.

ISAAC

Exactly.

NARRATOR

Water Malone is not important because watermelon is red. He matters because sometimes the person everybody overlooks is the person the whole field needs.

Corny is not interesting because corn is yellow. He matters because hurt feelings can change people.

General Rich Broccoli is not just the strong guy. He remembers what happened when his garden was attacked.

The food is funny. But the feelings are real.

SCENE 4 — THEN THE MAP GETS BIGGER

GLITCHBOT

Opening Realm Philadelphia map.

DEORUM

Again?

GLITCHBOT

Yes. You survived the tutorial. Congratulations.

NARRATOR

Once the player starts looking, the game expands.

The city matters. The houses matter. The buses matter. The water lines matter. The electrical system matters. The restaurants matter. The gardens matter. The libraries matter. The builders matter. The teachers matter. The people cleaning up matter. The people delivering food matter. The people repairing the building matter.

The city stops being background art. It becomes part of the game.

SCENE 5 — THE CITY IS ALIVE BECAUSE PEOPLE WORK

ISAAC

Think about your house. You turn the faucet on. Water comes out. You turn the light on. Light comes on. You open the refrigerator. Food is cold.

DEORUM

Hopefully.

ISAAC

Hopefully. But none of that is magic. Somebody made it work.

GLITCHBOT

Plumbers. Electricians. Utility workers. Construction workers. Drivers. Store workers. Farmers. Repair technicians. Sanitation workers.

ISAAC

Exactly. Now imagine learning all of that while you're running around trying to save the city.

DEORUM

That's better than homework.

ISAAC

Don't tell your teacher I said that.

SCENE 6 — THE PLAYER DOES THINGS

NARRATOR

The player is not supposed to stand around waiting for a hero. The player learns to do things.

Carry something. Fix something. Plant something. Read something. Deliver something. Cook something. Help somebody. Organize something. Find the route. Learn the system. Defend the garden. Clean the block. Solve the problem.

And sometimes, yes, blast a sugar monster directly into next Tuesday.

Balance.

SCENE 7 — HEALTH WITHOUT A LECTURE

DEORUM

So the game tells you what's healthy?

ISAAC

Sometimes. But the point ain't to yell at people about what they ate.

GLITCHBOT

Shame-based nutrition system rejected.

NARRATOR

Food has culture. Food has family. Food has money attached to it. Food has access attached to it. Food has memories. Food has recipes. Food has neighborhoods.

The Great Food Fight can teach better choices without pretending everybody lives in the same house, has the same money, eats the same food, or has the same options.

The mission is not: be perfect. The mission is: learn more, build more, and make better choices when you can.

SCENE 8 — FAMILY IS PART OF THE GAME

NARRATOR

And none of this begins in a laboratory. It begins in family life.

Morning routines. School runs. Music. Dinner. Snacks. Mess. Cleaning. Stress. Ideas. Bedtime. Trying again tomorrow.

DEORUM

And losing one shoe.

GLITCHBOT

Historical event confirmed.

ISAAC

GLITCHBOT

Correct.

NARRATOR

The family is not a loading screen before the adventure. The family is part of why the adventure exists.

SCENE 9 — COMMUNITY ISN'T BACKGROUND EITHER

ISAAC

If there's a garden giving out food, that's part of the world. If a library is teaching kids, that's part of the world. If somebody is publishing a book, that's part of the world. If a restaurant makes something special, that's part of the world.

The game can connect children to real kinds of work and real kinds of places. Not by turning the story into an advertisement. But by showing that a city is full of people making things happen.

Restaurants. Gardens. Community spaces. Events. Challenges. Recipes. Learning missions. New things to do.

GLITCHBOT

Expansion accepted. Canon sale rejected.

SCENE 11 — WHY MAKE IT A VIDEO GAME?

NARRATOR

Because children already understand games.

Maps. Levels. Rewards. Characters. Teams. Unlockables. Challenges. Progress. Fail. Try again. Learn the pattern. Get better.

The Great Food Fight asks: What if some of that same energy helped children understand food? Or money? Or trades? Or transportation? Or publishing? Or gardens? Or teamwork? Or the city itself?

SCENE 12 — THE WHOLE MAP

DEORUM

So what's the point?

ISAAC

You get the whole map.

DEORUM

What does that mean?

ISAAC

It means nobody tells you life is only one thing. You can make music and learn construction. You can make games and grow food. You can read books and learn business. You can draw characters and learn science. You can learn your city and still imagine another universe.

GLITCHBOT

Specialization acknowledged. Unused potential rejected.

SCENE 13 — WHAT WE ARE ACTUALLY FIGHTING

NARRATOR

The enemy is not candy. Candy is just fun to throw at.

The bigger fight is against things that drain people. Bad information. Bad systems. Bad habits. Hopelessness. Waste. Neglect. Giving up. Thinking you're too young to learn. Thinking you're too old to change. Thinking somebody else has to fix everything.

The game takes those giant problems and turns them into missions small enough to play.

One garden. One book. One meal. One repair. One route. One child. One block. One mission at a time.

GLITCHBOT

Health. Homes. Restoration. Food. Knowledge. Work. Dignity. Imagination. Community.

Turning pain into lessons, trash into gardens, paperwork into maps, work into dignity, and children into builders.

The Great Food Fight. Learn the food. Learn the city. Protect the garden. Build something better.

And when the vegetables start yelling, pay attention. They're probably trying to tell you something.
